SO THIS WAS WORTH ASKING SEPARATELY FROM THE CLOSED SIZE-BAND QUESTION, and the board
now says why: bands are a piecewise-constant approximation to exactly this structure,
whereas an intercept models it directly and with fewer parameters. The board's own
standing objection - "the template already charges a fixed cost per opening, the code
adder" - does not cover it either, because the adder is a constant per code that sits
OUTSIDE the supply term and cannot vary by bucket.

Four arms, everything else held identical, the adder untouched, leave-one-JOB-out:
    A  median rate through the origin (CURRENT)              13.06%  median  7.32%  18/29

    C  least squares a + b x area, per code, pooled bands    13.74%  median  9.50%  15/29
    D  B but only where the fit beats the median in training 13.80%  median  8.14%  17/29

ALL THREE LOSE AND NOTHING IS CHANGED IN THE ENGINE. The absolute figures are quoted
with their caveat: this harness omits the supplier correction and takes a different
fallback path, so its arm A reads 13.06 where the real engine is 12.28. Arm-to-arm is
what it is for, and a 0.8-point offset cannot flip B's 2-point gap.

C IS THE INTERESTING FAILURE and the reason the decision was not made on the mean. At
0.68 behind, better on 16 documents and worse on 13, it looks arguable. It is not,
because of WHERE the losses land. It fixes precisely what the hypothesis predicts -
Favell House +21.9 -> -9.2, ASHE +27.9 -> +9.1, Georgie's -27.3 -> -21.1, Whitsbury
-7.3 -> +0.6 - and then wrecks the money: Brandon earlier +26.5 -> +51.2, Brandon
Elkins +9.9 -> +30.4, Zelltec Crownhill +17.2 -> +30.0. The three Brandon revisions
are 89% of the priced money in the archive. An intercept fitted over a narrow range of
areas EXTRAPOLATES, and Brandon is 2,200 units of closely similar sizes.
