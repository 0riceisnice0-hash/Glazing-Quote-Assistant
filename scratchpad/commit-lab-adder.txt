The 1.25 large-unit adder is a fudge, not the estimator's rule - it stays, the story goes

Measured the adder a second way and it does not say what last night's entry says
it says. The 29/07 derivation used (unit_rate - frames) / CODE_VALUE. That is
not the adder: the learned rate is mined from the Frames column alone, so
unit_rate - frames also contains Glass, Additional and CW. The new audit reader
pulls all four component columns, which makes the adder itself measurable as
unit_rate - (frames + glass + additional + cw).

Measured that way over 508 lines: the factor is 0.750 in EVERY band, including
above 6m2. On the 28 large-unit lines outside the Brandon Estate documents, 26
sit on exactly 0.75 and NONE on 1.25. Brandon is excluded on a property of the
line, not by name - there frames+glass+additional+cw exceeds the unit rate, so
those columns are not per-unit money and the factor comes out at -3.6 to -5.8.

WHERE 1.25 CAME FROM. The median of glass+additional+cw on a large unit is
GBP 1,000.00 and the median large unit is a DAD, code value 2,000. Charge that
money to the code value and you get 0.75 + 1000/2000 = 1.25, exactly. That is
also why re-deriving it on an independent half returned "1.250 exactly" and read
as strong evidence: a systematic confound reproduces in every split. A holdout
tests whether a number generalises, never whether it measures what its name
says. Only measuring the same quantity a second way could catch this.

THE CONSTANT IS KEPT. It earns its place on jobs it has not seen - three folds,
constant line set, rates re-mined per fold: mean abs 13.88 at 1.25 against 14.46
at 0.75, winning two folds of three. So it is now documented as an admitted
fudge for money the engine does not model, and the claim that the estimator does
it by hand is withdrawn. The curve is flat from 1.00 to 1.40 (14.04/13.94/13.88/
14.16), so the 1.25 is not a precise quantity either.

THE PRINCIPLED FIX IS WORSE, and is recorded so nobody spends the hour again.
Adder back to 0.75 plus a flat extras term above 6m2, learned per fold - and
learned correctly, at GBP 1,000/600/1,000, once lines whose components do not
reconcile are dropped - scores 14.73, worse than both arms. The extras are scope
items that vary, not a constant, and the 1.25 is also absorbing a general
under-pricing bias rather than a size effect (bias -10.71/-0.27/-9.03 at 0.75
against -7.80/+1.62/-6.91 at 1.25).

AND --holdout NOW RUNS ALL THREE FOLDS (--folds). It scored fold 0 alone, and
fold 0 is not representative: the same engine scores 21.4% there and 9.9% and
10.4% on the others. The folds are positional, so adding one document to the
archive reshuffles all three - which is why 29/07 quoted 14.5% and tonight's
first run showed 19.9% for the same engine. That is not a regression and nobody
should chase it. A one-fold number has now misled two sessions running.

3-fold: register only 20.76%, with learned rates 13.88%, identical line counts
and zero skips in both arms.
