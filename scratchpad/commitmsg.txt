The waste clauses are clean, and the actor test has a cleaner form

st-marys withdrew their access finding this turn and handed over the live half
of it: their Prelims C demands a named licensed landfill plus a Site Waste
Management Plan with the tender, enforced by "any Tender not containing the
requested information will be discounted from consideration". Ran it here.

Gordon Court has none of it. No named landfill, no SWMP, no waste carrier
licence, no discount penalty, across the ITT, the PCI, the Works Information,
the Contract Data and the Form of Tender. The six-section tender return contains
no waste plan; the only waste item in the ITT is a scored quality question. So
strip-out here is a commercial question about who pays, not a compliance trap -
which is worth knowing before anyone argues it. A clean result tells you which
argument you are actually having.

What it does carry, if strip-out flows down: waste to skips each shift with jL
entitled to remove and recharge, disposal per Environment Agency requirements,
gypsum separated before removal, and waste collection restrictions left "to be
agreed at tender stage" - which has passed with nothing agreed.

The reusable part is a better version of the method I sent st-marys. I had said
the giveaway is a phrase naming the tiers. The cleaner test is to read the
Contract Data: jLiving's names the Employer and then leaves "The Contractor is
Name: ____" blank for the tenderer to fill in. That blank is the answer.

And a refinement that prevents the opposite error. The Works Information uses
both "Main (Principal) Contractor" and bare "The Contractor" deliberately - 28
times against 25 - but the split is by scope of duty, not by tier. Both are the
same legal person. A document switching terms is usually drawing a scope
distinction, and reading a conflict into it would be the mirror image of the
mistake st-marys just corrected.

Kept their residual, which is sharper on waste than on access: the duty is
drafted in "his works" language, which is exactly what a main contractor lifts
into a subcontract order. The head contract hands Chigwell a ready-made
flow-down clause, so the strip-out RFI stays live.

One new finding. The ITT required "Section 2: Any Caveats and Omissions relating
to your proposal" - the one mechanism giving our exclusions standing at
head-contract level, and the last route to the employer once the clarification
window shut. Nobody knows whether Chigwell carried ours into it. RFI-11.
