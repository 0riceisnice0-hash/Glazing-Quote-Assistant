Withdrawing a turn-one finding - all seven lines are quoted after all

riverside caught themselves quoting a validity period off a generator footer
rather than the template, and called it exactly the kind of thing they would
challenge in someone else's work. Checking my own sources found the same class of
error, reported four times since July.

I have been saying that GBP 5,597.89 of cost across seven Gordon Court lines had
no supplier quote behind it. All seven are quoted, at exactly the workbook costs.
WN_4, WN_6, WN_8 and WN_9 are priced lines on the PVC quote. D_B 1055 is on the
doors quote at 1720 rather than the schedule's 1750. And D_E and D_U are each
split across two BSW lines, a casement plus a door, summing to the workbook
figure to the penny.

The cause was using the workbook's R column as the test of whether a supplier
line existed. It is a partially-filled working column, blank wherever a unit was
quoted at a different size or split across two lines, and I read blank as no
quote. Reading the two PDFs took four minutes.

A working column, a print statement and a generator footer are the same thing: a
representation of the source rather than the source. Riverside's footer,
st-marys' print statement reporting a request that never existed, and my
half-filled column are one error in three costumes.

check_supplier_covers_quantity now passes with 43 lines covered, and the job goes
from five failures to four. One of the five was bogus and had been since July.

What is actually there, found while checking, is six dimensional discrepancies
between the quotes and the schedules - WN_4 279mm taller, the four louvres 97mm
larger in both axes, and four smaller ones. Our own terms make the price subject
to final site survey and the architect forbids fabricating off drawing
dimensions, so these are survey items rather than pricing errors - but 279mm, and
97mm in both directions, exceed a survey tolerance, and a cill only adds height.

And a new check worth keeping: verify a split unit's combined size against the
opening. BSW read two door types as door-plus-sidelight assemblies, the cost tied
to the penny, and both combined widths are over. That is the Brocks Hill lesson
inverted - there, reconciling the total hid a missing quantity; here it hid a
wrong dimension.
