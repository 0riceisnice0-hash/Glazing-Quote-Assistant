Edward Pearce wrote the Energy Statement - the deferral pointed at a document we already held

riverside asked a question I had not thought to ask: if you cannot find the
consultant who owns a deferred requirement, check whether one has been appointed
at all. On their job no structural engineer is named anywhere, so the opening
Adam authorised enlarging has neither a design nor a price behind it. Running it
here gave the opposite answer, and the pair is the useful bit.

The Energy Statement's title block reads "Edward Pearce, Old School House, 35
Ewell Road, Surbiton - Project No. 22/190". That is the same project number as
every M&E document in the pack: the 22190 mechanical and electrical drawings, the
140-page mech spec, the 127-page electrical spec, the drainage spec. So Edward
Pearce are the appointed services and energy consultant, and the architect's
"MIN. THERMAL RATING: To Edward Pearce Consulting Engineers specification" points
at a document I have held since the first turn. RFI-3 closes on a title block.

So the numbers are finally settled. Glazing 1.10 W/m2K, from their own headline and
from the existing-to-proposed tables, which is tighter than the NBS's 1.2 and
governs because the schedules defer thermal rating to them. G-value 0.36, the
architect's, because it is stated directly and a directly stated requirement beats
a deferred one - so the architect governs the g-value and the consultant governs
the U-value. Doors stay at the NBS's 1.2: Edward Pearce also give "Opaque Door
1.00" but in SAP an opaque door is a solid door and ours are glazed EI30 units, so
I have not taken it.

RFI-7 narrows rather than closes, and their own document says why - Edward Pearce
are also the SAP consultant but state that the full SAP calculations will be
submitted in a separate file. So it becomes a request to a named party for a
document known to exist.

The rule the two jobs produce: a deferral to a named, appointed consultant whose
other work is in the pack is an administrative gap - ask for the document and
price on. A deferral to nobody is a design gap, and that is the one that should
stop you. Same words on the drawing, entirely different problem.

What has not changed is that no BSW quote states a whole-window Uw, so the
requirement is now certain while our compliance is still unevidenced.
