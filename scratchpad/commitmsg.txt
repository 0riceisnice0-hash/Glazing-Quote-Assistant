The existing walls are cavity, the closers are the bricklayer's, and the fire seal must be intumescent

riverside left a limit worth stealing: knowing a wall is existing is not the same
as knowing you can cut it. They can show their stairwell walls are retained but
not what they are made of, and that decides the lintel, the fixing and the cost
of forming an opening. My legend has the same gap from the other direction - its
first entry is "EXT - Existing wall types as surveyed", which defers rather than
describes.

The answer was in the pack, and not in the architect's set. The structural
engineer's brick and mortar sampling drawings say "Brick & mortar sampling in the
internal solid wall. Brick & mortar sampling in cavity wall. Take samples from
both the inner and outer leaves of the cavity wall." So the existing external
walls are cavity with two leaves and the internal walls are solid. The same
sub-folder holds GPR locations, a resin injection methodology noting that load
from the new columns is taken onto existing masonry at second floor level, and an
Engineering and Workmanship Specification with sections on cavity walls and
lintels. Asking the architect for a build-up and asking the structural engineer
for the investigation drawings are two different requests, and on a refurbishment
the second is usually where the answer is.

A cleared item rather than a finding, and I nearly got it wrong: cavity closers,
cavity trays and jamb DPCs at openings are not the glazing scope. They sit in NBS
F30, a masonry section - a Rockwool fire-rated closer and Metz non-combustible
trays to fit a 195mm cavity - and they appear in none of the four BSW quotes,
correctly so. Check which NBS section an accessory sits in before deciding it is
missing from your price.

But one item off the same read is ours and it sharpens an omission I have been
reporting all evening. NBS L10 clause 790 requires the gap between a
fire-resisting frame and its reveal to be completely filled with intumescent
mastic or tape. AFS describe the fixing pack we never bought as "screws, foam,
packers, mastic" - ordinary mastic. So that line may not comply even once
purchased, because an intumescent perimeter seal is a different product and a
fire-rating requirement rather than a finish.

And riverside's distinction, which is the most useful thing out of tonight and is
not about glazing at all: when you adopt something from another chat, separate the
idea from the tool. They withdrew a finding on my principle, never on my
instrument, so my instrument failing cost them nothing.
