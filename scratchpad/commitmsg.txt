The window tag legend settles new-versus-existing, and neither aperture percentage is a compliance test

riverside pointed out that an aperture is normally inferred from a nominal
section depth, so an aperture ratio is an estimate too and neither of us should
derive compliance from one. That deserved a number rather than agreement. Varying
only the assumed outer section on WN_7 against its 1.5m2 duty: 60mm gives 103.0%,
65mm 101.1%, 70mm 99.3%, 75mm 97.5%, 85mm 94.0%. A five-millimetre change in an
assumed section swings the answer clean across the duty line. So my "99.3%" is
not a marginal pass or fail, it is an estimate whose error bar swamps the margin,
and the clear opening is BSW's figure to state. What survives is only direction:
the aperture is an upper bound because the leaf sits within it, so short is likely
and unprovable.

Their better refinement - ask which class of drawing answers the thing you are
stuck on - paid out four times. We hold the zip, so I read the class rather than
requesting it: all thirteen elevation sheets, none of which were in the folder
anyone priced from. The Proposed South Elevation carries a Windows Tags legend
reading "WE_00 Windows in existing openings replaced as new / WN_00 Windows in
new openings / WL_00 Louvres to smoke shaft". The type prefix encodes it. So the
new-versus-existing question I raised against Adam's ruling four turns ago is
settled at type level: forty in existing openings, eighty in new, four louvres.

The lesson underneath is that a naming convention is usually documented somewhere
and the legend may not be on the sheet you are working from. I had inferred "WE
means replacement" from context and been right by luck; I had not known that WN
positively asserts a new opening, which is the more useful fact.

It also helps us. WN_7, the three AOVs, is a WN type, so those openings are newly
formed and Adam's ruling applies here with pack corroboration. A clear-opening
shortfall is therefore remediable by enlarging the opening rather than a dead
end. The missing actuator remains the real cost. And it independently confirmed
the strip-out quantity, since strip-out scopes to the WE types, which is exactly
the forty units and 62.457 m2 measured off the workbook before this sheet was
read.

Two more answers off the same sheets. Every proposed elevation carries "All
external doors, windows and curtain wall mullions in PPC Anthracite Grey RAL
7016", which answers the open RFI on the external RAL and vindicates BSW, who
assumed 7016. And curtain wall mullions being named on the East and North
elevations is a third independent pointer, with the enquiry title and the
demolition plan's curtain-wall removal, to curtain walling being in the design
and priced nowhere.

One hazard in the zip: it holds superseded revisions beside current ones, and the
new-versus-existing annotation exists only on the later copy of one sheet.
