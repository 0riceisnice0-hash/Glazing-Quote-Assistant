An addendum supplier is not a frame supplier - SUPPLIER noise down from 18 to 13

Second half of the board's triage job. Two false-alarm classes, both of which
would have sent Adam looking for money that is exactly where it should be.

CRESTWOOD PARK, reported -14,223.25 at ratio 0.658. Its block reads BSW
27,329.60 / Teleflex 14,223.25 / total 41,552.85, and its Frames column totals
27,329.60 - the BSW figure EXACTLY. The reported gap IS the Teleflex line to the
penny. Teleflex is not framing; it is a separate scope item that appears as an
ADDENDUM ROW below the priced lines, and it is the same Teleflex the footing
checks already know about by name (it was on the original false-alarm list for
Crestwood and Feltham). Frames cannot contain it, so comparing the frames total
against a block total that includes it is asking the wrong question.
  The check now tries the SUBSETS of the block before reporting anything. If the
frames total matches any subset, the document reconciles and the suppliers left
out are addendum suppliers - it reports nothing. Only a frames total matching NO
subset is a real question. Preferring the largest matching subset means a
genuine multi-supplier reconciliation like Gordon Court's BSW + Aluminium Fire
Systems is still tested against both, not explained away by one.

ZELLTEC CROWNHILL UPVC, reported at ratios of 8.268 and 22.139 - numbers that
cannot mean anything and should never have been printed. That document lists
three suppliers, Tru Frame / Strongdor / BSW, with NO cost against any of them.
So the block never met the empty row that was supposed to stop it, the six-row
window ran on into the first priced line, and its Additional cell was read as the
buy. Stopping at the first empty row was necessary but not sufficient: a row now
also has to LOOK like a supplier row, meaning a NAME in the column straight after
'Supplier used:'. The block's total row leaves that cell empty; a priced row has
a number in it, because that is its Additional column. Requiring name-or-empty
excludes the priced row and both Zelltec findings disappear.

NEXT IMPROVEMENT FOR THIS CHECK, recorded rather than done: percentage is the
wrong discriminator. Grange Hill is a REAL catch at 1.8% while six findings sit
nearer 1.00 than that and mean nothing. What made Grange Hill real is that its
gap equals a WHOLE UNIT on a line - seven windows priced where BSW quote eight,
at GBP 419.32 each. Testing whether the gap is an integer multiple of some line's
unit frames cost would separate a missing unit from rounding, which a tolerance
band cannot do in either direction.
