The install line pays for fitting, and Luton Airport is airside

Jacob asked whether the GBP 14,157.24 issued to Ryebridge on 13/07 was built on
one mobilisation, after Adam agreed on 30/07 to split the works into two visits
at no cost. The answer is that it was built on none.

The workbook is two lines: 3 x SMA Shopline double door at 4,219.08 (BSW supply
2,315.01 + DAD code 2,000 x 75% + 404.07 additional - the house rule, correct)
and INSTALLATION 1,500.00, which is exactly 3 x the DAD labour code of 500. The
labour codes are fit-only per unit. They carry no mobilisation, no travel, no
supervision and no site attendance, so there was no allowance to spend twice.

The exposure is the wording, not the money. Every operative on an airside job
needs an LLA induction for clearance and is escorted throughout, and the
proposal puts inductions, clearance and escorted access in the executive summary
as things "to be coordinated prior to installation" - not in the EXCLUSIONS
column, where a client looks. That names who arranges it and is silent on who
pays, and silence reads as included. Defensible on this job, which has 7,212.21
of headroom over the BSW buy; not defensible on the next package off the same
framework.

New rule check_site_access_is_priced_or_excluded: a non-glazing site cost -
escorted or inducted access, security clearance, permits, live premises, phased
visits, night working - is either a priced allowance or an exclusion. A sentence
promising to coordinate it is neither, and the selftest asserts that. 9/9
variants green, whole suite still passes.

Also recorded, from sampling the archive rather than from this job: 21 of 35
issued proposals promise a CSCS/SMSTS supervisor in full-time attendance plus a
dedicated project manager and a separate contracts manager. Boilerplate, and
unfundable against a 1,500 labour line.

The other 25 of the 28 queued work orders already carry a route and were left in
place for the bridge to deliver.
