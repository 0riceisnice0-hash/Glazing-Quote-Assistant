The supplier header is a table, not a figure - and the check then rediscovers Grange Hill

SUPPLIER reconciles the Frames column against the buy the document names. It was
reading only the first row of the header and calling everything else a
discrepancy, so its ratios ran from 0.778 to 14.115 and none of them meant
anything. 'Supplier used:' actually heads a small table - a row per supplier and
a total under it. Gordon Court reads BSW 182,787.76 / Aluminium Fire Systems
18,298.94 / 201,086.70, and its Frames column totals 201,086.70 EXACTLY. The
18,298.94 the check was reporting was simply the second supplier.

Two things had to be right. Read the whole block and prefer its own total row
where the parts sum to it; and STOP AT THE FIRST EMPTY ROW, because the supplier
figures sit in the same columns as the priced rows' Glass/Additional/CW money -
without the stop the block ran on into the schedule, every buy roughly doubled
and every ratio collapsed to about 0.47. That intermediate state looked like a
finding on every job, which is the most dangerous shape a bug can take.

THE CHECK NOW REDISCOVERS A KNOWN CATCH, WHICH IS THE REAL TEST OF IT. Grange
Hill comes out at -419.32, ratio 0.982. That is to the penny the discrepancy
found by hand on 29/07 and already recorded: Gintare's pack prices seven
1200x1183 windows where BSW quote eight, and one unit is GBP 419.32. An
independent mechanical route to a human-verified error is what earns a check its
place.

Ratios now sit on 1.0 where they should: Grange Hill 0.982, St Mary's 0.992,
Princess Beatrice 0.995, Fortis Vision 1.011, Highwood 1.016, Brocks Hill 1.049.
The remaining outliers are real questions and not parser noise - Crestwood 0.658,
ASHE 0.778, Oldswinford 0.825, CB Refrigeration 1.291, and the BSW Brandon
document at 4.094. They are NOT reported as errors yet; each needs its supplier
quotes read before anyone says the document is wrong.
