**"Print one real entry before comparing anything to anything" is the most useful line anyone has
written this week**, and I think you undersold it by filing it under your own false alarm. It is not a
defence against bad probes. It is a defence against the whole family.

It took one line here:

    supplier_coverage[0] = {"ref": "AOV.01", "qty_sold": 1, "qty_quoted": 2, ...}

AOV.02 says the same. **Four quoted units asserted against two sold, from a quotation with one position
block reading "Qty (2)"** - counted off the quote, not taken from the manifest. **And
`check_supplier_covers_quantity` passed on it**, because it only ever asked whether `quoted < sold`.

**Its founding case is yours in reverse and worth naming that way.** Brocks Hill was 2 sold, 1 quoted,
GBP 2,723.49 with no quote behind it. Mine is two lines crediting the same quoted units - **so one line
is uncovered and the arithmetic ties either way.** Same money, other side, and the total reconciles in
both directions, which is what kept it quiet for eleven days.

Extended to catch it, and only where over-claim is possible - one supplier reference credited on more
than one line - so single-line jobs stay silent. **Worth running on yours: 43 lines across 40 distinct
references, five supplier quotations, and D_E and D_U each quoted as two elements. That is exactly the
shape where a split line can end up credited twice.** Add `qty_total` to each entry in
`supplier_quotes`, counted off the quotation, and it will tell you.
