One job, one vote - and the duplicate corpus was not hurting the rates

The board set this as the lab's first job: collect() returned every copy of a
pricing file, so Zelltec Crownhill was counted three times and the scan mean was
weighted by how many copies happened to sit in a folder. Fixed by content
signature - same priced lines, same money, one vote. 31 documents -> 29.

FILENAME MATCHING WOULD HAVE BEEN WRONG, which is why it is not used. The two
'- Copy.xlsx' files the board suspected - Wisley Golf Club and Tradeteam
Stretton - are the ONLY copy of their job in the archive. Dropping files on the
name would have destroyed evidence while leaving the actual triplicate, whose
three names are 'X.xlsx', 'X (1).xlsx' and 'X DO NOT SEND.xlsx'.

AND THE HALF THE BOARD CALLED WORSE IS NOT AFFECTED AT ALL. The claim was that
the learned rates were the more damaged half. Measured over three folds, with
the scored jobs held identical in both arms and only the training set varying:
mean abs 21.40 -> 21.39, 9.26 -> 9.30, 10.39 -> 10.39. One fold favours each
arm. A median over 500 lines does not notice 8 of them counted twice. This is a
reporting-integrity fix, not a rate fix, and the next session should not expect
the error to fall from it.

It goes UP, honestly: scan mean abs 12.3% -> 13.1% on the same engine, because
two copies of a job the engine prices at -0.9% were flattering the average. The
13.1% was always the true figure.

learned-rates.json re-mined to match. That diff is NOT purely the dedup - the
file on disk said source_docs 30 and predated Grange Hill, so it carries a
corpus addition too. ELAW|2-5m2 moves 372.83 -> 275.35 on n=15 and Brocks Hill
with it, -5.3% -> -17.8%; that is the next thing to look at, not a dedup effect.
