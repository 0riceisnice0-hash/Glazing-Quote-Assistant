READ THE DOOR SCHEDULE TOO - AND WHEN A U-VALUE SAYS "AREA WEIGHTED AVERAGE", DO THE ARITHMETIC.

Two lessons from finally opening St Mary's door schedule 2376-08, which nobody had read in three turns
of work on that job.

1. A U-VALUE CAN BE STATED IN MORE THAN ONE SCHEDULE, AND THE DOOR SCHEDULE USUALLY SPLITS IT. On St
   Mary's the window schedule says 1.4 and the energy annex says 1.3 windows / 1.2 doors - I had those.
   The door schedule independently carries BOTH: per-door notes on D.01, D.17, D.22 and D.26 reading
   "Door to achieve min u-value of 1.4W/m2k", AND a general note "External Doors U-value 1.2 w/m2k".
   So the 1.4 requirement exists in three separate documents and does not depend on the energy annex at
   all - which matters, because "the sustainability appendix does not apply to our package" was the
   obvious way for a client to make the problem go away. Open the door schedule even when your package
   looks like windows.

2. "AREA WEIGHTED AVERAGE" IS A REAL DISTINCTION AND IT CUTS BOTH WAYS. The full note reads: "External
   Doors U-value 1.2 w/m2k. Note this is an AREA WEIGHTED AVERAGE u-value - not a centre pane value."
   SM5 Wexham's rule says do not reject an element against an average - I had the basis recorded as
   "per element" and that was wrong for the doors. But the answer is to COMPUTE the average, not to drop
   the point. On 22.078 m2 of Smart Wall Pocket at SMA's published 1.8 plus at most 20.367 m2 of MC600,
   the MC600 would have to hit 0.55 W/m2K for the package average to reach 1.2. Even a generous 1.0 on
   the MC600 gives 1.42. The finding survived the correction, and it is now a much stronger thing to put
   to a client than an assertion about one element. If you cannot compute it, you cannot claim it either
   way - but do the sum before you drop a finding OR raise one.

AND A THIRD, ABOUT SUPPLIER SILENCE. The schedule says "No locking mechanism or latch" / "Non-lockable
device" on four external doors. Bellview quoted concealed panic bars on all seven units without comment.
Aplus, quoting the same scope, wrote on their own quote: "It is unclear what a Non-Lockable Device is,
quoted all doors with Panic Bars." Same ambiguity, same default, and only one supplier said so. When two
quotes cover the same scope, read the one that ASKS QUESTIONS for the questions - it is telling you where
the specification is unclear, and the silent quote has the same exposure without the warning. Aplus also
did this on Riverside (free area) and Georgie's showed the opposite failure mode. Treat a supplier's
written caveat as free scope review.

RELATED, ON REPLACEMENT JOBS: triage's John North Hall note says to check strip-out, making good and
disposal before relying on our proposal's exclusions. Ran that on St Mary's - only the strip-out lands on
us (SOW 1.09 cross-refers into our item 6.01). Making good and decoration are carried separately by the
client as SOW section 8, so our "Internal Finishing" exclusion is safe there. Worth actually checking
rather than assuming either way; the answer differed by item on the same job.
