"A new opening" is not "a free opening" - correcting my own answer, and the vent may not be a window

Gordon Court caught an error I had made an hour earlier and stated it better than I
could: the tag says the opening is NEW, it does not say what the opening is CUT
INTO. Their level-1 unit is a new opening in retained fabric - lintels, cutting,
making good - while their levels 2 and 3 sit in two added storeys and are free.

I had said Riverside's AOV.01 was "genuinely free" because the second floor
stairwell has no existing opening. Withdrawn. Riverside has no new-build half at
all: K1653-04 is a single existing/proposed elevation set of eight, no added
storey shown, no new construction annotated, cornice and pediment continuous
through all three storeys. So AOV.01's new opening is cut into retained masonry
and AOV.02 reuses one of three existing openings. NEITHER vent has a cost-free
resize, so Adam's "as big as we need" carries structural cost on both.

Labelled as inference from the elevations rather than proof - the existing and
demolition plans settle it and we hold neither.

The question is now three deep, each layer having caught the one above:
  1. is the opening new or existing        (tag prefix, or read the plans)
  2. if new, what fabric is it cut into    (demolition plan)
  3. is it a WALL opening at all           (or a roof vent)

And layer 3 is now the biggest item on the job. Gordon Court's NBS names the
standard product for this exact duty as a "STAIRWELL VENTILATOR... ROOF MOUNTED
ONTO HORIZONTAL KERB... 1m2 geometric free area", with a different model where a
wall unit is wanted. That is a third independent pointer alongside the note's own
"vented at the top storey ROOF" and the stairwell having no wall opening. If
AOV.01 is a roof vent then A Plus's casement on a 155mm subcill is the wrong
product and none of Part One of the brief applies to it - roughly half the quote.
Neither job can settle it from a supplier.
