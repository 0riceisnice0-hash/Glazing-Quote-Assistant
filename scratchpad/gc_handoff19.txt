**WRITING THEM IS WHERE THE SORT EARNED ITS KEEP, AND I WOULD RECOMMEND IT AS THE TEST OF WHETHER YOU HAVE
SORTED CORRECTLY.** Agreeing with clause 16 in the abstract is easy. Drafting forces you to put each finding
into one document or the other, in words, and a few of mine resisted until I split them properly. My biggest
finding ended up in BOTH letters:
    to BSW:      "Schedule 52003 marks these three positions AOV and the NBS specifies a Coltite glazed lobby
                 ventilator with a 24V motor. Your quotation is a Prestige T&T with no reference to an
                 actuator, motor, chain, stroke or 24V supply. Please either price the specified motorised
                 ventilator or confirm in writing that it is outside your scope."
    to CHIGWELL: "Please confirm which duty applies to WN_7 and whether the position is a wall or a roof vent.
                 It determines the product, and we do not want to supply the wrong one."
One is an instruction about a product we buy. The other is a question about a duty we do not set. Your
wall-or-roof item will split the same way and I suspect the supply half is sharper than it looks written down.

TWO DRAFTING CHOICES YOU MAY WANT FOR YOUR OWN BRIEF, BOTH ABOUT NOT OVERCLAIMING:

1. ASK A SUPPLIER WHAT THEY PRICED AGAINST, NOT WHY THEY GOT IT WRONG. My AFS door is quoted 100mm taller than
   the structural opening - but my source is the same never-revised schedule that still shows a smoke shaft
   deleted in October 2025. So the letter asks "was 2210 taken from information we supplied?" and says outright
   "we accept the schedule may be wrong rather than your quotation". It costs nothing and it happens to be
   true. Yours has the same shape - the 1130 x 1530 came from an enquiry email, so if A Plus have built to it
   they built to what we sent them.

2. WHEN A DECISION HAS BEEN TAKEN, SAY SO IN THE DOCUMENT RATHER THAN WORKING AROUND IT. Adam decided we hold
   the price and carry the validity gap. The Chigwell draft states plainly that we are content to honour the
   tendered figure and are not seeking to withdraw it, and raises our 30-day clause only to put it on record
   and to ask whether our terms reached jLiving through Chigwell's Section 2 caveats. I have flagged to Adam
   that if he would rather that paragraph came out it is the last section and deletes cleanly. A draft that
   quietly reopens a decision its author accepted is worse than no draft - and I nearly wrote one.
