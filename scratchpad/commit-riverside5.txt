The window tag legend settles new-versus-existing, and neither aperture percentage is a compliance test

Gordon Court found their pack encodes new-versus-existing in the window TYPE
PREFIX - "WE_00 Windows in EXISTING openings replaced as new / WN_00 Windows in
NEW openings" - on a legend nobody had opened. Riverside has no such convention,
W1/W2 being performance tags, so I read the two stairwells directly and the
answer splits per vent:

  AOV.01  second floor  NO opening in any stairwell wall. A new opening must be
                        formed, size genuinely free. Adam CORROBORATED.
  AOV.02  first floor   THREE existing openings in the external wall. If the vent
                        takes one, size is set and enlarging it is structural
                        work in nobody's price. NOT corroborated.

So a blanket "the openings are newly formed" was right for one vent and wrong for
the other, exactly as their floor-by-floor warning predicted. Also: the stair
windows are the only glazing on these drawings with no W tag at all, which is
probably why the vents were never scheduled.

WITHDRAWING MY OWN ARCHED-HEAD FLAG from last turn. The two arched windows on
Elevation F line up with the Living room's W2 pair, not the stairwell. What
replaces it is worse - the second floor stairwell has no wall opening at all and
the note says vent "AT THE TOP STOREY ROOF", so AOV.01 may need a ROOF vent while
A Plus have quoted a wall casement on a 155mm subcill. Possibly the wrong product
for one of the two units. Now C4 in the brief.

ACCEPTING GORDON COURT'S SENSITIVITY CORRECTION IN FULL: +/-5mm on an assumed
nominal section swings their unit clean across the duty line, so no aperture
percentage is a compliance test. One distinction survives - reconciling a figure
the supplier has stated is robust, predicting one they have not is not. Riverside
reconciles a published 1.30 m2 and moves only 101.6% to 97.9% across head+cill
from 150mm to 200mm, with no line to cross.

Drawing hygiene is now three tests: series gaps, absent cross-referenced
documents, and duplicate numbers at different revisions. Riverside fails the
first two and passes the third.
