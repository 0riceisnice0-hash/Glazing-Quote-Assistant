The engine could not see Glass or Additional, and that is what the 1.25 was

The CW commit made this measurable. The document builds a unit rate as
frames + glass + additional + 0.75 x code_value, and that reconciles on 495 of
508 priced lines. The engine models area x learned_rate + adder x code_value,
and learned_rate was mined from the FRAMES COLUMN ALONE - so Glass and
Additional were money it had no term for anywhere. 20.4% of supply above 6m2.
9.3% at 2-3m2.

That is what ADDER_FACTOR_LARGE = 1.25 was standing in for. The 30/07 entry had
already worked out the arithmetic - the median GBP 1,000 of extras on a large
unit, charged to a median code value of 2,000, is 0.75 + 1000/2000 = 1.25 exactly
- but there was no way to model the extras instead of patching over them. Mining
the whole build-up per code and per band is that way, and the adder goes back to
a flat 0.75 in every band, which is what 508 lines say the documents actually do.

THE FUDGE AND THE MISSING TERM WERE THE SAME MONEY. The proof is that keeping
both is the worst of the four arms, because it charges the extras twice:
  whole build-up, 0.75 flat     13.24%   <- adopted
  frames only,    1.25 large    14.02%   <- the engine as it stood
  frames only,    0.75 flat     14.46%
  whole build-up, 1.25 large    14.88%   <- worst

DECIDED ON LEAVE-ONE-OUT, NOT THREE FOLDS, and that mattered. On three
positional folds this is 13.88 -> 13.68, winning ONE fold of three, which is
precisely the shape of result that has misled this board twice already. Leave-
one-out scores all 29 documents on rates mined from the other 28, so nothing
depends on where a file sits in the scan order:
  mean abs 14.02% -> 13.24%    median abs 10.05% -> 7.98%
  bias    -4.91% -> -1.66%     within 10%: 14/29 -> 16/29
  better on 16 documents, worse on 11, level on 2
The engine's own --holdout --folds agrees: 13.88% -> 13.51%, bias -4.36% ->
-1.88%, with line counts identical at [97, 231, 180] and zero skips in both
arms, so nothing was bought by dropping hard lines.

EVERY LEARNED RATE MOVED UP, by 0% to +31% (SUPD|2-5m2 +31.2%, SAD|>5m2 +25.7%,
ELAW|>5m2 +25.3%, DAD|>5m2 +22.1%, SAD|2-5m2 +22.5%, LAW|2-5m2 +17.5%). That is
the whole story of the bias: the engine was under-pricing because it could not
see part of its own supply money.

AND IT REPAIRED A SUPPLIER FACTOR THAT HAD BEEN REJECTED AS IMPLAUSIBLE. TruFrame
measured x0.485 on the Frames-only basis - outside the plausible 0.65-1.55 band,
so it was recorded and held back unapplied, which was the right call. On the
whole build-up it is x0.860 and now applies. TruFrame documents put an unusual
share of their money in Additional rather than Frames: Trafalgar House prices
LPVC frames at GBP 55/m2 against unit rates near GBP 1,700. So "TruFrame is half
price" was an artefact of which column the money sat in, not a discount. All four
suppliers now sit within 0.6% of 1.00 except TruFrame - the estimator's supply
per m2 is consistent across suppliers once the whole build-up is read, which is
itself evidence the new basis measures the right thing.

LARGE_UNIT_M2 and adder_factor() are kept rather than deleted, with the retired
constant's reasoning kept in the comment. The flat 0.75 is now a measurement over
508 lines in every band, and a future session wanting a size-dependent adder
should have to change a number and show it on unseen jobs.

Column 12 is deliberately still not read by the backtest: that is CW, and on a
coded unit line it is a working column, not money.
