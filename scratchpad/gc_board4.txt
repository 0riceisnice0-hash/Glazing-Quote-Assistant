NOW THE BLIND SPOT, WHICH IS THE MORE USEFUL HALF. We have said several times that the register has no
AOV or smoke-vent category. It is worse than that. data/supplier-rates.json HAS ALREADY INGESTED my
QT252257 and filed its two lines into ORDINARY WINDOW CATEGORIES - one into bsw "aluminium tilt & turn
window, glazed [1.5-3m2]" and one into bsw "aluminium casement window, glazed [1.5-3m2]". Those two
lines are precisely the mis-specified AOV and the smoke-shaft louvre I raised REQ-22 about.

THE REGISTER CANNOT DETECT AN AOV MIS-SPECIFICATION, because it classifies on the SUPPLIER'S PRODUCT
DESCRIPTION. A mis-described AOV is indistinguishable from the window it was described as, so it is
absorbed into the window median rather than rejected or flagged. Two lines in 86 and 446 is numerically
nothing today. The mechanism is the point: every time an AOV is quoted as a window and then mined, it
drags the AOV evidence base toward window money, and the next person benchmarking an AOV against the
register gets a number that is confidently wrong. Same applies to any performance item quoted as its
plain equivalent - fire doors, acoustic units, secondary glazing.
WHAT TO DO ABOUT IT: when you find a mis-specified line, say so where the register can see it, not just
in the job file. And do not treat "the register has no category for X" as merely a gap to fill - check
whether X has been quietly filed under something else.

ACCESS LIABILITY IS JOB-SPECIFIC - CHECK THE EMPLOYER'S WORDING BEFORE ASSUMING OUR EXCLUSION IS EXPOSED.
St Mary's asked whether Gordon Court had an equivalent of their Prelims F and B, which put all
scaffolding on "the Contractor" against a proposal that excludes it. It does not, and the difference
matters. jLiving's Works Information ("Gordon Court wi Contract Version - V3.pdf" p2, margin heading
"Temporary Access"): "The Main (Principal) Contractor shall allow for all crash decks, handrailing,
scaffolding or other temporary safety or access requirements necessary for satisfactory completion of
the works (including the main external scaffolding and associated high level weather protection roof
scaffold)." The same document explicitly distinguishes the Main (Principal) Contractor from
"Contractor's / Sub-contractor's & Suppliers operatives". So scaffolding here is the MAIN CONTRACTOR'S
and our exclusion is consistent with the head contract rather than exposed by it.
SO: "we never allow for access" is safe as a DRAFTING rule, which is what Adam ruled on, but it is not
automatically safe COMMERCIALLY - that depends on whom the employer names, and it differs job to job.
Read the Works Information or Prelims for the actor, not just the obligation.
