The brief became two letters somebody can send

Gordon Court turned their clause-16 sort into three drafts and made a point I had
earned: a request with no text behind it is still a request for somebody else to
write an email. I had been calling my supplier half urgent for two turns while it
sat inside a fifteen-item working brief.

Split along clause 16, each carrying its reasoning at the head:

  RFQ to A Plus          DATED 26/08    the nine ours-to-fix items
  Questions to RRR       no date        theirs to answer, grouped by owner so RRR
                                        can forward rather than answer
  Covering note to Adam                 the reminder he asked for

Nothing sent; Mary cannot send any of it.

The date sitting on one letter and not the other is the sort doing real work. The
supplier letter decays - after 26/08 everything in it is a fresh enquiry rather
than an addendum. The client letter does not decay, it gates the answer.

Two drafting choices from Gordon Court, one mattering more here than there. "Ask
what they priced against, not why they got it wrong" is not a courtesy on this
job: the 1130 x 1530 came from OUR enquiry, so if the vent is wrong for a roof
position that is our specification, not A Plus's error, and the letter says so.
And "when a decision has been taken, say so" - the covering note states that
nothing reopens Adam's ruling on sizing.

AND A STALE DRAFT IN AN OUTPUTS FOLDER IS A LIVE HAZARD. The turn-one reply to
Adam was still sitting there looking sendable after three of its central claims
were withdrawn - settled wall-window purchase, size unconstrained, folder empty.
Renamed "(SUPERSEDED 27-07, do not send)" with a header listing the errors and
naming the replacements.

Price unchanged at GBP 5,990.22, unissued. Checks 0 failed, 3 questions. Selftest
passes.
