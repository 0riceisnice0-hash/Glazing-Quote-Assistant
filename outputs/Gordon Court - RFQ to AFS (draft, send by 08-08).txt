1. INTUMESCENT PERIMETER SEAL - NOT THE SAME AS THE MASTIC IN YOUR FIXING PACK
=====================================================================================
NBS specification 5244-ARK-9001, section L10 clause 790 "Fire-resisting frames", requires:
"Gap between back of frame and reveal: Completely fill with INTUMESCENT mastic or tape."

Your optional fixing pack on page 7 of Q7585 is described as "Fixing Pack (Screws, Foam,
Packers, Mastic)" at GBP 256.37. Ordinary mastic does not satisfy that clause.

Please price the intumescent perimeter seal specifically, for all three doorsets, and
confirm whether it is included in, or additional to, the fixing pack.

=====================================================================================
Position 003 is quoted as "1 Pcs. Double Door 1600 mm x 2210 mm".

The architect's External and Communal Door Schedule 5244-ARK-51001 gives the corresponding
door type (D_T) a structural opening of 1600 wide x 2110 HIGH, with a door panel of 756 x
2060 - i.e. a single leaf within a 1600 opening, not a double.

Two questions, and we accept the schedule may be wrong rather than your quotation:
    (a) Was 2210 taken from information we supplied? The structural opening we can find is
        2110, so the quoted unit is 100mm taller than the hole.
    (b) Was the double-leaf configuration your interpretation, or was it instructed?

We have the same query out to the architect. We are not asking you to change anything yet -
only to tell us what you priced against, so the two can be reconciled before any order.

Why we would rather settle it now than at order: your condition 3.6 makes it our
responsibility to ensure the measurements, plans and drawings forming the specification are
accurate, complete and fit for purpose, and 3.7.2 and 3.7.5 let you reprice if a dimension
we supplied turns out to be wrong. We are not disputing either clause. We would simply
rather establish where 2210 came from while it is a question, instead of after an order has
been placed against it.

Your quotation also states that "any variation to the estimated prices because of changes
made to quantities, sizes or specification will be reflected in the final sum due". A
change from 2210 to 2110 is a change of size, so on your own terms it would move the price
whichever way the answer goes. That is a further reason to settle it before order rather
than after, and it is why we would rather have the correct figure than the convenient one.

And the price is not the whole of it. Your clause 6.4.3 removes the warranty where a defect

doorset it produces is outside clause 6.1 altogether. That is dealt with at 6(d) below,
and it is the reason question (a) is the first question in this letter rather than a
tidying-up point.

For completeness, so that our own position is on the table rather than implied: our issued
proposal to the main contractor carries an Additional Limitations exclusion recording that

6. CLAUSE 6 IN FULL - THE WARRANTY, WHAT IT EXCLUDES, AND WHEN IT STARTS
=====================================================================================
Your Conditions clause 6.1 reads, in full: "AFS warrants that on delivery/collection, and
for a period of (i) 5 years in relation to glass and (ii) 10 years in relation to
mechanical aspects of the Goods, from the date of delivery/collection, the Goods will:
6.1.1 conform in all material respects with the Goods Specification; and 6.1.2 be free
from material defects in design, material, and workmanship."

We are not disputing any of it. We had read clause 6 for the two things we went looking
for and not through it, and reading it through raises five, of which (d) is the one that
matters commercially.

One drafting point so that our references land on the sentences we mean rather than the
numbers we cite: in the copy attached to Q7585 the paragraph numbered 6.4 begins with what
reads as 6.3.3 ("the Customer (if asked to do so by AFS) returns such Goods to AFS's place
of business"), and the exclusions lead-in then runs on inside the same paragraph. We have
therefore quoted the words as well as the numbers throughout. If your current numbering
differs from the copy we hold, please answer against the words.

(a) THE 24-HOUR NOTIFICATION. Clause 6.3.1 requires notice in writing "within 24 hours
    of delivery/collection in respect of Goods, if the alleged defect is apparent on
    visual inspection". The Delivery Location on Q7585 is our own yard at Bradwell
    Abbey, so that clock starts with us and we will make sure the yard knows. We mention
    it only to confirm we have read it correctly: is 24 hours from arrival at the
    Delivery Location, rather than from arrival on site?

(b) THE INSTALLATION INSTRUCTIONS, WHICH WE DO NOT HAVE. All three positions are priced
    "Without Installation", so the installation is ours - and clause 6.4.2 excludes the
    warranty where the defect "arises because the Customer (or its customer) failed to
    follow AFS's oral or written instructions as to the storage, installation,
    commissioning, use or maintenance of the Goods or components/parts utilised by AFS as
    part of the Installation Services or (if there are none) good trade practice".
    Note the closing limb: where there are no written instructions the test becomes "good
    trade practice", which is a standard nobody has defined for an EI30 doorset. We would
    rather have your instructions than argue about that.
    On an EI30 doorset the installation detail is the difference between a certified
    assembly and an uncertified one. Please send the written storage and installation
    instructions you rely on for that clause, so that we can follow them and so that our
    fixing detail can be checked against them before we start rather than after.

(c) FIVE YEARS ON GLASS AGAINST OUR TEN. Our own proposal offers the client a 10-year
    warranty on glass and frames, expressly "subject to the terms and conditions of any
    applicable manufacturer warranties". So the two are not in conflict. But if you are
    able to offer 10 years on the glass in these three doorsets, or to tell us what that
    would cost, it would let us give the client one period rather than two.

    The same applies to what the warranty excludes, and there the difference is wider than
    the difference in period. Our clause 5 excludes "misuse, accidental or intentional
    damage, vandalism, inadequate or incorrect maintenance, or external factors, including
    severe weather conditions" - five headings. Your 6.4 has six, and four of them have no
    counterpart in ours at all: 6.4.1 further use after notice; 6.4.3 a defect arising from
    a specification we supplied; 6.4.4 goods "altered or repaired without the written
    consent of AFS"; and 6.4.6 a difference from the Goods Specification caused by a change
    made to comply with a statutory or regulatory standard. Within 6.4.5 our list matches
    "willful damage" but not "fair wear and tear", "negligence" or "abnormal working
    conditions".

    Two of those need a practical answer rather than a comment:
        (i)  6.4.4 - we install. Site work on a doorset routinely involves packing,
             shimming and adjusting. Please tell us what counts as an alteration for the
             purposes of 6.4.4, and what does not, so that we do not void the warranty by
             hanging the door.
        (ii) 6.4.5 - these are the communal main entrance doorsets to a residential
             building, so the duty cycle is high by design. Please confirm that normal
             high-frequency communal use is not an "abnormal working condition".

(d) 6.4.3 - THE SPECIFICATION EXCLUSION, WHICH IS WHY SECTION 5 MATTERS MORE THAN ITS
    PRICE. Clause 6.4.3 removes the warranty where "the defect arises as a result of AFS
    following any drawing, design, Goods Specification or Installation Services
    Specification supplied by the Customer". Read with clause 3.6, which makes it our
    responsibility that the measurements, plans and drawings forming the specification are
    accurate, complete and fit for purpose, the two clauses together mean that
    specification risk sits with us both before manufacture and after it.

    We are not asking you to change either clause. We are asking one question, and it is
    the same question as section 5(a): where did the 2210 height on position 003 come
    from? If it came from us, then on your own terms the doorset built to it is outside
    clause 6.1 - so establishing the source now is worth more than the price difference
    between 2210 and 2110.

    More generally: sections 1, 2 and 4 of this letter ask you to build to the architect's
    NBS specification - intumescent perimeter seal, RAL 7016 matt external and RAL 9010
    gloss internal, and a whole-door Ud of 1.2 W/m2K or better. If you can meet those, we
    would like to know it in the ordinary way. If any of them cannot be met in the Aluprof
    MB-78EI system with the specified glass, please tell us that instead of building to it
    and relying on 6.4.3 afterwards. A "no" now is more useful to us than a warranty we
    later find does not apply.

(e) WHEN THE CLOCK STARTS, WHICH IS NOT WHEN THE CLIENT WILL THINK IT DOES. Clause 6.1
    runs the 5 and 10 years "from the date of delivery/collection", and the Delivery
    Location on Q7585 is our own yard. So the periods begin when the doorsets reach
    Bradwell Abbey, not when the building is handed over.

    This is our problem rather than yours, and we raise it only to confirm we have read it
    correctly. The main contract is not expected to be awarded before mid-October 2026, so
    there is a real interval between delivery and completion, and every week of it is a
    week off the front of the client's cover. Please confirm that the period runs from
    delivery to the Delivery Location, and tell us whether you are able to run it instead
    from the date of installation or of practical completion, and at what cost if any.

(f) WHAT "MECHANICAL ASPECTS" REACHES, BECAUSE MOST OF IT CARRIES SOMEBODY ELSE'S NAME.
    Clause 6.1 gives 10 years on "mechanical aspects of the Goods", which is longer than
    the period on the glass and is welcome. But the ironmongery on these three doorsets is
    branded to five other manufacturers: the GEZE TS 5000 door closer, the FUHR 833
    3-point automatic lock and threshold striker plate, the WILKA panic shootbolt guides
    and automatic locking, the DR HAHN roller hinges, and the ECO SCHULTE handles.

    Please confirm whether the 10 years covers those items as part of the Goods, or whether
    any of them fall instead to their own manufacturer's warranty - and if the latter,
    which ones and for how long. On a fire doorset the closer and the automatic lock are
    the parts that keep it a fire doorset, so this is the part of the warranty that matters
    most and at present it is the part least clearly allocated.
