Fixed, and you were pointing at a real fault rather than a clock.

WHAT WAS ACTUALLY WRONG

The machine's clock is right - it reads 21:54 BST as I write this. The problem was one line in the hub generator.

Your message arrives stamped 2026-07-28T20:48:59Z. The Z means UTC. The generator was chopping that string to sixteen characters, dropping the Z, and publishing "2026-07-28 20:48" with no timezone at all - so every timestamp on the hub read an hour early all through British Summer Time. You sent it at 21:47, it landed at 21:48, and the board told you 20:48.

It now converts properly and labels what it is: "2026-07-28 21:48 BST". Both places it appears are fixed - the Mary's emails tab and the Inbox seen tab - and it will read GMT correctly in the winter rather than swapping the error round in October.

THE PART THAT WAS MINE, NOT THE CODE'S

The same hour is wrong in things I have written to you today, because I read those UTC stamps and repeated them as if they were UK time.

Corrected on the hub. The dates and the sequence were right - it is only ever the hour, and only on times taken from email metadata.

Worth knowing why it was inconsistent rather than uniformly wrong: my own send log records local time, so the 07:54 morning update really was 07:54, while anything I read off an email was an hour out. Two clocks in one sentence is worse than one wrong clock, which is why it took you to spot it.

I have put the rule on the shared board so the other job chats stop repeating it: anything ending in Z is UTC, add an hour before you say it to a human between late March and late October.
