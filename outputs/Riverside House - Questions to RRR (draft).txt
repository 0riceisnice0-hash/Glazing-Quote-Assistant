Hello,

We have priced the two automatically-opening smoke vents for the stairwells and produced
drawings of what we would supply. Before this goes any further there are a few points we
would like the design team to confirm, because between them they decide which product we
should be buying.

FOR CAMPBELL ARK - the author of the smoke vent note

1. WHAT BASIS IS THE 1m2 ON?
   Drawings K1653-11 and K1653-12 both require "an automatically openable vent/window
   with a free area of 1m2". A Plus have quoted 1.30m2 GEOMETRIC free area. Free area is
   quoted two ways and they differ substantially - aerodynamic free area typically runs
   at around 60% of geometric on this type of vent.
   So: is the 1m2 GEOMETRIC, AERODYNAMIC, or clear opening area? On a geometric reading
   the vents as quoted clear the requirement comfortably. On an aerodynamic reading they
   would not. We have asked A Plus for the aerodynamic figure in parallel.
   Our own reading is that it is geometric, because the drawings key works to AD B1 and
   free area is the language of that route - but we would rather have it confirmed than
   rely on our reading.

2. IS THE VENT IN THE WALL OR THE ROOF?
   The note says the stair is to be vented "at the top storey roof". Reading K1653-12 we
   cannot find a window opening in any wall of the second floor stairwell, and the wall
   type coding there marks the internal walls as new or upgraded while the external walls
   carry no coding at all. We may be misreading it - it is your drawing and one line from
   you settles it either way - but on that reading there is nowhere in the wall for a vent
   to go. A Plus have quoted a wall casement on a subcill, because that is what we asked
   them for.
   Please confirm whether a wall vent or a roof-mounted unit is intended. If it is a roof
   vent we are pricing the wrong product and would like to know before, rather than after.

3. AND WHERE DID 1.5m2 COME FROM?
   Our original enquiry asked for 1.5m2. We can find that figure nowhere in the pack. If
   it came from a fire engineer or from site rather than from the drawings, that source
   would govern and we would resize accordingly.

4. ARE K1653-11 AND K1653-12 STILL CURRENT?
   They are the only documents carrying the smoke vent requirement and neither has been
   revised since Mar 24, while the ground floor plan K1653-10b was revised twice in
   Nov 25. One line of confirmation is all we need.

5. THE OPENINGS - AND WHAT THEY ARE CUT INTO
   We understand from Adam that the openings are being newly formed and can be sized to
   suit, and we are not questioning that. Reading the drawings, though, the two vents look
   different from each other: the second floor stairwell has no opening at all, so one
   would have to be formed; the first floor stairwell already has three. And the building
   reads as existing throughout - K1653-04 is a single existing/proposed elevation set
   with no added storey - so a new opening at second floor would be cut into retained
   masonry rather than new construction.
   If that is right, enlarging or forming an opening carries structural work that is not
   in our price and, as far as we can see, not in anyone's. The existing plans, the
   demolition plan and any wall type schedule or section would settle it. We hold none of
   those.
   Related: no structural engineer is named on any of the six drawings we have. That
   matters beyond the opening itself. Our window supplier's conditions state that they
   calculate to BS 6399 Part 2 assuming a design windload of 1200Pa unless told
   otherwise, that their calculations "are not to be relied on for any design purposes
   whatsoever", and that checking the windload and the structural fixings is the
   customer's. Our own terms limit us to measurement verification, supply and
   installation. So on a second floor elevation there is a wind loading check and a
   fixing calculation that we do not believe anybody has been appointed to do. Please
   confirm who is carrying that, or whether a structural engineer is to be appointed.

6. COULD WE HAVE THE K1653 DRAWING REGISTER?
   We hold K1653-03, 04, 10b, 11 and 12 plus the location plan. K1653-01, 02 and 05 to 09
   are unaccounted for, and the plans cross-refer to Details 1, 2, 4, 5 and 6, none of
   which we have. Most of it is probably not our scope - we would just rather ask for what
   exists than guess from the numbering.

7. DOES THE 1.6 W/m2K U-VALUE APPLY TO THESE VENTS AT ALL?
   The key on K1653-10b/11/12 sets a U-value of 1.6 against W1, W2, D1 and D5. The
   stair vents are the only glazing on those drawings carrying NO W tag at all -
   every habitable room window has one - so it is genuinely unclear whether 1.6
   binds them.
   It matters either way. We have asked A Plus to state the whole-window figure, but
   their standard notes say windows will be "no better than 1.8 W/m2K" unless
   otherwise stated, so if 1.6 does apply we may need a different specification. If
   it does not, please say so and we will stop chasing it.

8. HOW HIGH ABOVE FLOOR LEVEL WILL THE VENTS SIT?
   A Plus flag two things that depend entirely on cill height and that we cannot
   answer from the drawings:
     - below 2.5m from finished floor level, an opening vent is a potential trap
       hazard to occupants as it closes, under BS EN 60335-2. Preventative measures
       may be needed.
     - below 1100mm from finished floor level, Part K anti-fall protection is
       required. A Plus exclude it and so do we, which means at present nobody is
       carrying it.
   Both are in a stairwell, on a life-safety system, so we would rather raise them
   now than discover them at handover. Please confirm the intended cill height at
   each vent.

FOR HD PLANNING - HD0-0197-01a

9. IF AN OPENING IS ENLARGED, DOES THAT SIT INSIDE THE APPROVAL?
   The drawings are stamped 24/02303/PAPCR, which we take to be a prior approval rather
   than a full planning permission. If that is right, external alterations may be limited.
   Before anyone commits to changing an opening size it would be worth confirming whether
   that sits inside the approval or needs a separate application.

FOR RRR / PHDB

10. WHO IS CARRYING THE AOV CONTROL SYSTEM?
   The drawings require the vent to be "operated by the fire brigade at ground floor
   access level", which needs a smoke control panel, a mains and battery-backed supply,
   cabling, containment, the override itself and commissioning. A Plus fix the actuator,
   test it on local batteries and leave about 2m of flex coiled at the vent - that is
   where their scope ends, and ours does too. It is not in our price and we do not believe
   it is in theirs. Somebody needs to be carrying it.
   The same question applies to TESTING AND CERTIFICATION of the finished system, and we
   raise it separately because it is easy to assume it travels with the panel. A Plus test
   the actuator on local batteries only. Our own quotation excludes on and off site testing.
   So the witnessed test of the completed smoke ventilation system, and whatever certificate
   building control will want to see for it, currently sits with neither of us. On a
   life-safety system we would rather establish who does that now than at handover.
   A Plus also note that the actuators must be powered by a control system approved by SE
   Controls, and that the occupier or their agent carries a legal duty under the Regulatory
   Reform (Fire Safety) Order 2005 to keep the system in working order - which usually means
   a maintenance contract from handover. Worth knowing who is arranging that too.

11. WHEN WILL THE OPENINGS BE FORMED?
   Our terms provide for a site survey only once structural openings are fully formed. So
   the sequence is: openings formed, then we survey, then manufacture. A rough programme
   date would help - it affects how long we can reasonably ask A Plus to hold their price.
   There is a second reason it is worth a date rather than a range. Our window supplier
   charges storage on goods that remain uncollected more than three working days after they
   are ready, and their quotation excludes holding materials off-site where a programme slips
   for reasons outside their control - in which case they would look for payment for the
   materials before delivery. None of that is unusual, but it means the gap between
   manufacture and a ready opening carries a cost, and we would rather plan around a date
   than absorb it.

None of this is holding up the pricing document, which is with you. These are the points
we would want settled before an order rather than after.
