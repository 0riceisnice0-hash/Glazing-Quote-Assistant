ONE THING IN A PLUS'S TERMS THAT IS WORTH A MINUTE OF YOUR TIME

Reading their conditions rather than their prices: A Plus state that it is the customer's
responsibility - ours - to ensure the Building Regulations are complied with, and that
they "do not warrant or represent that any Product supplied shall comply" unless they have
expressly said so on the quotation. Our own terms do the opposite: clause 16 says we are
not responsible for regulatory strategy and rely on the client's professional team.

Neither document is unusual and I am not suggesting either is wrong. But this product
exists to satisfy one regulation - Part B - and that is precisely the responsibility we
have disclaimed to the client and accepted from the supplier. If the 1m2 turns out to be
aerodynamic and the vent as quoted does not meet it, that gap is where the argument would
happen.

The fix is free and it is pre-order: their clause has a door in it - "unless where
expressly stated to the contrary by the Supplier" - so the letter now asks A Plus to state
the aerodynamic free area and the EN 12101-2 classification ON the revised quotation
rather than in a reply, and to confirm the whole-window U-value the same way. Asking costs
a line before an order and is a variation afterwards.

SOMETHING I HAD WRITTEN DOWN AS OUR PROBLEM AND IT IS LARGELY NOT

I told you above that A Plus charge storage on goods left uncollected more than three
working days after they are ready. I recorded that as a cost we would carry while we wait
on PHDB. Having read our own terms rather than just theirs, that is wrong in our favour:

  - our Inclusions say installation is priced against the final agreed programme and that
    "any delay outside of Fenster's control may incur additional costs";
  - Cancellation and Postponement lets us recover additional costs incurred if the client
    postpones after materials have been procured, which is exactly what a storage charge
    is;
  - and Supplier Delays says we are not liable for additional costs caused by a
    third-party supplier.

So a slip driven by the building works is recoverable rather than absorbed - but only after
there is a contract and only if it is on our terms. The clause turns on the client postponing
"the contract" after "procurement of materials", and we have neither. Two separate phases,
and I put them the wrong way round first time:

  BEFORE an order  the delay is your decision to wait for PHDB, not RRR postponing anything,
                   and it costs nothing, because we would not order from A Plus until RRR
                   have ordered from us and their storage clock only starts at manufacture.
  AFTER an order   a client-driven slip is an additional cost incurred following procurement
                   and we can recover it, provided the order is on our standard terms.

Worth knowing before you agree a date with RRR rather than after, and worth watching if they
come back wanting to contract on theirs.

We offer clients a ten-year warranty on glass and frames. A Plus give us twelve months on

The letter now asks A Plus what the frame and glass warranty is as distinct from the

I then went back over that comparison properly, and three things came out of it.

THE FIRST IS IN OUR OWN TERMS AND IT IS NOT ABOUT THIS JOB. Our Guarantee clause offers ten
years and never says ten years from what. There is no start date in it - the only "from the
date of" anywhere in the document is the thirty days on quotation validity. Ten years from
order, from delivery, from completion of installation and from practical completion are four
different promises, and an undated one is generally read against whoever wrote it. That is
one sentence to fix, it applies to every job rather than this one, and it is a decision about
what we sell, so it is yours. It is the one item on this list that costs nothing and needs
nobody's agreement.

THE SECOND IS THAT A PLUS'S TWELVE MONTHS STARTS EARLIER THAN IT LOOKS. It runs "from the
date of delivery completion" - delivery to us, not to site or to handover - and this job is
waiting on PHDB's building-works costs before it is even submitted. Meanwhile A Plus charge
storage on anything left with them more than three working days after it is ready. So their
storage terms push us to take delivery early and their warranty terms push us to take it
late, and the client's cover is what pays for whichever way we go. The letter now asks
whether the twelve months can run from installation or handover instead. Cheapest question
on the list and it had never been asked.

THE THIRD IS THE EXCLUSIONS, and it turned out to be wider than the period. Comparing the two
clause sets properly: five of the seven things A Plus exclude have no equivalent in ours. Two
of the five are live rather than academic - the actuator guarantee is conditional on our
following installation instructions we do not hold, and on the vent being powered by a
control panel approved by SE Controls that nobody has yet bought, priced or specified. If
another trade picks that panel on price, the moving part of a life-safety system may end up
unwarranted, and we would still be the name on the ten years. Both are now questions - to
A Plus, and to RRR alongside the question of who is carrying the panel at all.

Two corrections to what I told you above, in the interests of not overselling it.

I led with the 15,000-cycle limit and I should not have. Weekly testing is about fifty-two
operations a year, so fifteen thousand cycles is centuries - the twelve months will always
be reached first unless the vent is opened forty times a day. It is worth one line in the
letter, not the headline I gave it.

And I said "a nine-year gap" without checking that our own clause reaches the actuator at
all. It covers "all glass and frame products" - an actuator is neither. That is not better
news than the gap, it is different news: on the narrow reading the client has ten years on

less than it appears to, and either way it is worth a look at the wording.
