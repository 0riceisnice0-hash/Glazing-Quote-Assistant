PART TWO - NOT FOR A PLUS. THESE GO TO RRR / CAMPBELL ARK / THE FIRE ENGINEER
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
These have all come up since the vents were priced, and three of them could change the
answer to Part One. Worth asking before Gintare spends A Plus's time.

If only two things get asked, make them C0 and C2 - the fire strategy and the demolition
plan. Between them they answer most of the rest, and neither is something we can work
out from what we hold.

C0. FIRST, PLEASE SEND THE FIRE STRATEGY. There is no fire strategy drawing or report
    in anything we hold. The entire smoke-vent requirement reaches us as a single red
    note on two floor plans. Somebody produced a strategy in order to write that note -
    it will say in its own words whether the 1m2 is geometric, aerodynamic or clear
    opening area, and it will settle C1 without anyone having to reason from the
    drawings. On a comparable job the fire strategy legend simply stated the duty:
    "AOV. 1.5m2 CLEAR OPENING AREA." One document, and C1 is answered.

C1. IS THE 1m2 GEOMETRIC OR AERODYNAMIC? The drawings do not say. Our reading is
    GEOMETRIC, for two reasons: the drawings' own key works "TO AD B1", which is the
    prescriptive Approved Document B route and states stair vents as a free area,
    where aerodynamic is the language of the engineered BS 9991 / EN 12101-2 route;
    and comparable packs specify this exact duty as "1m2 GEOMETRIC free area" in so
    many words. On that reading the vents as quoted clear the requirement by 30% and
    nothing needs to change. Please confirm - or send the fire strategy per C0.

C2. ARE THE OPENINGS REALLY NEW - AND WHAT ARE THEY CUT INTO? Reading the two
    stairwells off K1653-11 and K1653-12:

      AOV.01, second floor stairwell (K1653-12) - the stairwell has NO window opening
        in any of its walls. Every wall around it is drawn solid, so a new opening does
        have to be formed here.

      AOV.02, first floor stairwell (K1653-11) - the stairwell already has THREE window
        openings in its external wall. If the vent takes one of them, the size is set by
        the existing opening.

    BUT "A NEW OPENING" IS NOT THE SAME AS "A FREE OPENING", AND ON THIS BUILDING IT IS
    NEITHER. What matters is what the opening is cut INTO, and two independent readings
    of your own drawings both say retained fabric:

      - K1653-04 is a SINGLE "EXISTING / PROPOSED ELEVATIONS" set of eight elevations,
        with no added storey shown and no new construction annotated, and the tower's
        cornice and pediment run continuously through all three storeys.
      - The floor plans colour-code every wall that is new or altered - new partition,
        new separating wall upgrade, separating wall upgrade to existing, blockwork
        infill. Around both stairwells the INTERNAL walls are coded. The EXTERNAL walls
        carry no coding at all on either floor, which reads as retained existing fabric.

    So AOV.01's new opening would be cut into existing masonry - lintel, cutting, making
    good, and a changed external appearance on an approved elevation. Neither vent has a
    cost-free resize and we should not price on the basis that they do. This is the one
    point where our reading of the drawings differs from what we have been told, which is
    why it is worth settling rather than assuming.

    ONE THING WE STILL CANNOT READ: what the external wall is actually MADE OF. Your key
    covers separating walls and partitions; there is no external wall build-up legend, so
    we know these walls are not new but not their construction. Solid or cavity changes
    the lintel, the fixing type and the cost of forming the opening.

    THAT IS USUALLY A QUESTION FOR THE STRUCTURAL ENGINEER RATHER THAN THE ARCHITECT.
    On a refurbishment somebody normally samples the masonry before anything is designed,
    and the answer sits in the engineer's investigation drawings - brick and mortar
    sampling locations, GPR surveys, that sort of thing - not in the architect's set.
    Please send whatever exists.

    AND A RELATED CONCERN WE WOULD RATHER RAISE NOW THAN LATER. No structural engineer is
    named anywhere on the six drawings we hold. The notes name a heating engineer and an
    electrician, and otherwise defer consistently to site: "CONTRACTOR TO ESTABLISH EXACT
    DRAINAGE LAYOUT", "TO BE SITE AGREED WITH HEATING ENGINEER/ELECTRICIAN & TO SUIT
    BUILDING INSPECTOR APPROVAL", "ELECTRICAL LAYOUTS ARE TO BE SITE AGREED WITH CLIENT".

    If that reflects the real position, then forming a new opening in retained masonry at
    second floor has not been designed by anyone, and "make the vent as big as we need"
    is committing to work that currently has neither a design nor a price. We are not
    raising this to be difficult - it is the difference between a variation we can cost
    and one nobody can.

    Please confirm per vent. And please still send the DEMOLITION and EXISTING PLANS:
    we hold neither, and on a comparable job the demolition legend was exactly where new
    openings were marked - "NEW STRUCTURAL OPENINGS. HEIGHTS TO BE CONFIRMED ON SITE."
    That drawing settles this properly; the above is read off floor plans, which is
    suggestive rather than conclusive.

    Worth noting alongside: the stair windows are the only glazing on these drawings
    that carries NO W1 or W2 tag. Every habitable room window is tagged. That may be why
    the vents were never scheduled, and it bears on item C6 below.

C3. AND IF THEY ARE ENLARGED, DOES THAT NEED PLANNING? The drawings are stamped
    24/02303/PAPCR, which is a PRIOR APPROVAL reference rather than a full planning
    permission. Prior-approval conversions normally carry tight limits on external
    alterations. Before anyone commits to "make them as big as we need", please
    confirm with HD Planning whether enlarging these two openings sits inside the
    approval or needs a separate application.

C4. IS AOV.01 A WALL WINDOW OR A ROOF VENT? This replaces an earlier query of ours
    about arched heads, which we now think was a false alarm - the two arched-head
    windows on Elevation F at second floor line up with the Living room's two W2
    windows, not with the stairwell.

    The real question is simpler and more expensive. The second-floor stairwell has no
    opening in any of its walls, and the note itself says the stair is to be "VENTED AT
    THE TOP STOREY ROOF". A Plus have quoted a wall window - a 1130 x 1530 casement on a
    155mm subcill. If AOV.01 is intended as a roof vent rather than a window in the
    stairwell wall, it is a different product from a different supplier and the quoted
    unit is simply the wrong thing. Please confirm which is intended, and if it is a
    wall window, which elevation it goes in.

    Three things point the same way and none of them is conclusive on its own, which is
    why we are asking rather than assuming:
      - the note's own words, "vented at the top storey ROOF";
      - the stairwell having no wall opening to put a window in;
      - and the standard product for this exact duty being a roof unit. On a comparable
        job the specification named a "STAIRWELL VENTILATOR... ROOF MOUNTED ONTO
        HORIZONTAL KERB... 1m2 geometric free area" - the same 1m2, mounted on the roof.
        The wall-mounted version in that same specification was a different model for a
        different position.

    If it is a roof vent, none of Part One applies to AOV.01 and we should be pricing a
    kerb-mounted unit, not a casement.
