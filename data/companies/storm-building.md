## THE LESSON - a dismissed notice is a hypothesis, not a verdict

On 27/07 Mary analysed the Contracts Finder award notice for the **Maternity Assessment Unit,
Guy's & St Thomas' NHS FT** (£240k main contract, GB-CFS-336915) and dismissed it on three
counts: dead (awarded 11/04/2025, ran to March 2026, published 469 days late), no glazing in
it (an internal maternity refurb; the phrase "the front door to maternity services" was a
metaphor), and wrong size/type.

**Adam then picked up the phone, and there was secondary glazing on it.**

Counts 1 and 3 were sound reasoning. **Count 2 - "there is no glazing in it" - was wrong**, and
it was the only one that mattered. The document scan could not see a secondary glazing package
that the client had; a two-minute call could. Mary's method was good and her conclusion was
still wrong, which is exactly why the call is the test and the desk analysis is only the
shortlist. **Do not close a Storm scheme on a reading of the paperwork.**

Genuine caveat that survives: the notice carries a Contracts Finder supplier ID rather than a
Companies House number, so that record alone does not confirm the legal entity. In practice it
does not block a relationship call - we have invoiced this company and been paid £43k, so the
entity we trade with is established by our own sales ledger. Confirm it before anything
contractual, not before a phone call.
