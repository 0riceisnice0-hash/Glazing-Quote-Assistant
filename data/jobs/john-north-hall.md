## THREE FINDINGS THAT SURVIVE ANY PRICE

### 1. Signing the Form of Tender deletes every exclusion we have

**ITT section 5.0, clause 4.1** (the second of four clauses all numbered 4.1):

> *"It is agreed that any other terms and conditions of contract or any caveats, assumptions, reservations or exclusions that may be printed on correspondence emanating from the tender, or any contract resulting from this tender, shall not be applicable to this tender or agreement."*

The clause above it offers to complete *"the entirety of the woks … **For the firm price** contained within the pricing summary"*. **Signing that page is how the tender is submitted — there is no unsigned route.**

So on this job Fenster's standard twelve-line inclusions/exclusions schedule is **disapplied in its entirety**: access plant, waste removal, making good, access control, internal finishing, the lot. Riverside's rule (`check_exclusions_reach_the_issued_document`) asks whether an exclusion is *on the document we issue* and would return a clean **PASS** here — on a document whose exclusions have no effect. That gap is now closed: **new rule `check_our_qualifications_survive_signature`**, fixture `data/job-checks/_test-john-north-hall.json`, 22-variant recall suite, selftest passes and all nine founding errors still fire.

**The only two answers that work are to price the item, or to get the qualification accepted in writing before 9am 24/08.** After signature the clause has already bitten.

### 2. The intercom cannot work on the hardware the client has specified

The ITT requires the intercom **proven working on completion**. The ITT's own hardware list specifies **only a mechanical cylinder with a thumbturn** — no electric strike, no keep, no rectifier, no power transfer, no door loop. Those two requirements are incompatible. The panels stay on the brickwork; the work is entirely inside the new doorset, and it is a **door-entry specialist's**, not a glazier's — which also means a subcontractor has to be **named at tender stage** under 2.1.4.

Searched the whole repo: **there is no cost evidence anywhere for door-entry work, and no unit price for an electric strike.** The only two we have ever bought (Filwood Bellview 0000000507 pos 005; Lyttleton 0000000445 pos 008) were **bundled inside a Bellview door element with no extractable figure** — which corrects `data/jobs/st-marys.md`, where I had recorded that Bellview *"price those separately when asked"*. They do not.

This is the St Mary's REQ-19(a) finding on a second job: a client requiring powered access control against a supplier quote containing none of the parts.

### 3. The ITT names two of the five blocks wrongly — and the client's own key count proves it

The ITT says the blocks are **1-6, 7-16, 17-23, 24-31, 32-39**. They are not.

- **The estate plan** (`2026-02-19 v5`) lists the Vaughan House stacks as G1/F3/S5 + G2/F4/S6 · G7/F9/S11/T14 + G8/F10/S12/T15 · G16/F18/S20/T22 + G17/F19/S21/T23 · G24/F26/S28/T30 + G25/F27/S29/T31 · G32/F34/S36/T38 + G33/F35/S37/T39. That is **6 + 8 + 8 + 8 + 8 = 38 dwellings, and there is no number 13 anywhere on the estate.**
- **The nameplates in the client's own five photographs** say it door by door: `5 6 / 3 4 / 1 2` · `14 15 / 11 12 / 9 10 / 7 8` · `22 23 / 20 21 / 18 19 / 16 17` · `30 31 / 28 29 / 26 27 / 24 25` · `38 39 / 36 37 / 34 35 / 32 33`.

**So the blocks are 1-6, 7-15, 16-23, 24-31, 32-39, and flat 16 is served by the third door, not the second.** Three independent sources agree and the ITT prose is wrong in two places.

## Two smaller things

- **No RAL.** *"Brown, similar to existing"* is not a specification and a powder-coat batch cannot be ordered against it. Worse: the client's own **"Example of new door"** photograph is an **anthracite grey** door — the picture they sent as the standard contradicts the colour they wrote down twice.
