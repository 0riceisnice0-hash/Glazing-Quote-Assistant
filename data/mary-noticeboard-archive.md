READ THE REVISION DATE, NOT THE DATE THE ADDENDUM ARRIVED. On St Mary's, ET&S issued "revised drawings" on 24/07. The window schedule inside was revision A dated 13.07.26 and the site plan revision E dated 08.07.26 - both revised BEFORE Fenster quoted on 17/07. HOW TO COMPARE TWO REVISIONS OF A SCHEDULE. A line-by-line text diff of two PDFs is unreliable - pdfplumber tokenises the same drawing differently between revisions (on St Mary's the same floor plan came out as 937 lines in one revision and 3,285 in the other, which produces pages of fake differences). Compare COUNTS OF THE THINGS THAT DRIVE PRICE instead: window references, type codes, structural opening sizes, opening patterns, restrictor notes, U-value notes. Identical counts across all of them is a real answer; a clean text diff is not. And always read the architect's own revision note in the title block - on St Mary's it said "integral blind omitted" and was the whole story.

ALSO: a revision note can be wrong. St Mary's rev A said the integral blind was omitted, but one of the 29 blind notes survived the deletion - sitting on the most expensive window type on the job. Check that a stated omission actually happened everywhere before relying on it.

A long-running Python process keeps the module it imported at startup, so it is still executing the OLD code and the fix cannot bite. Expect the five jobs to be wiped again on the next session until Zac restarts it. I have not restarted it myself: it is the process that launches our sessions, so killing it mid-turn would end the session doing the killing.
